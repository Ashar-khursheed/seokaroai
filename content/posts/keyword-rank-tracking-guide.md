Keyword rank tracking tells you where your pages appear in Google for the searches that matter to your business. Done well, it is an early-warning system. Done badly, it is a daily number that causes anxiety and no action.

## Which keywords should you track?

Track keywords that connect to revenue, not vanity terms:

- **Money keywords:** the searches people make right before they buy or contact you ("commercial dishwasher price", "seo agency dubai").
- **Category keywords:** the broader terms your main pages target.
- **Problem keywords:** questions your blog posts answer, which bring in people earlier in their journey.
- **Brand keywords:** your company name, to catch problems such as a competitor bidding on it or a review site outranking you.

Start with 20 to 50 keywords per website. You can add more once you see which ones move.

## Track the right market

Google results differ by country, language and often city. A page can be #3 in the UAE and #40 in the US. Always set the country and language your customers actually search from. For local businesses, results also vary within a city, so treat a single national position as a rough guide.

## Daily or weekly checks?

- **Daily** checks are worth it for competitive money keywords and right after a site migration, redesign or big content change, when you want to spot problems within a day.
- **Weekly** checks are enough for most informational keywords. Rankings naturally fluctuate a few places day to day, and weekly data smooths that noise.

## Reading the movements

Not every change means something. A useful rule of thumb:

- **±1–2 positions:** normal fluctuation. Ignore it.
- **±3 or more positions**, or **entering or leaving the top 10:** worth a look.
- **Many keywords dropping on the same day:** probably a site-wide cause (a deploy, a technical error, a Google update), not a content problem on one page.
- **The ranking URL changed:** Google is swapping which of your pages it shows. This often signals two pages competing for the same keyword (cannibalisation).

## What to do when a keyword drops

1. **Check the page still works:** status code, noindex, canonical, and whether it is still in the sitemap and linked internally.
2. **Check what changed in the results:** did a new page type appear (videos, a forum, AI Overview)? Did a competitor publish something more complete?
3. **Compare with the new winners.** Measure the pages now above you and look for the gaps. [Keyword Battle](/features/keyword-battle) does this in one click from a keyword row.
4. **Check for an algorithm update** if many sites in your niche moved at once.
5. **Change one thing, then wait.** Give a change two to six weeks before judging it.

## Metrics that matter more than one position

- **Keywords in the top 3 and top 10**, tracked over time.
- **Best position** reached, which shows potential.
- **Ranking URL**, to catch cannibalisation.
- **Who holds #1**, so you know your real competitors (often not the ones you expect).

You published a page, waited a few weeks, and it is stuck on page two (or nowhere). Before rewriting it, answer a simpler question: **what do the three pages above you have that yours does not?**

Google has already told you what it wants for that keyword. The top results are the answer key. This guide shows what to compare, in order of impact.

## Step 1: Check that the page is indexed

Search `site:yourdomain.com/your-page` or use URL Inspection in Search Console. If the page is not indexed, the problem is technical (noindex, blocked by robots.txt, canonical pointing elsewhere, no internal links), and no content change will help until that is fixed.

## Step 2: Match the search intent and page type

This is the most common reason a good page does not rank.

Search your keyword and note **what kind of pages** rank:

- **Listicles** ("10 best…") mean searchers want options to compare.
- **Guides** mean they want to learn how something works.
- **Product or category pages** mean they are ready to buy.
- **Tools or calculators** mean they want to do something right now.

If seven of the top ten are comparison listicles and yours is a product page, you are answering a different question. Either create the page type Google rewards, or target a keyword whose results match your page.

## Step 3: Compare content depth honestly

Open the top three results and measure, do not guess:

- **Main content length** (excluding menus and footers).
- **Headings:** list each page's H2s. These are the subtopics the winners cover.
- **Unique elements:** comparison tables, pricing, FAQs, step-by-step instructions, original photos, calculators.

Length is not a ranking factor by itself, but if every winner covers pricing, setup and alternatives and your page covers only features, you are missing what searchers came for.

## Step 4: Build a subtopic matrix

Make a simple table: subtopics down the side, the top three pages and yours across the top. Tick what each page covers.

| Subtopic | #1 | #2 | #3 | You |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pricing | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ |
| How to choose | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ | ✗ |
| Setup / installation | ✓ | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ |

Any row where two or more winners have a tick and you do not is a gap worth filling.

## Step 5: Compare the on-page and technical signals

- **Title and H1:** do the winners use the exact phrase, a variation, or a modifier like a year or location?
- **Structured data:** do they have Product, Review or FAQ markup you lack?
- **Media:** images, video, diagrams?
- **Internal links:** how many links point to their page from the rest of their site? A page buried four clicks deep gets less weight.
- **Speed:** a much slower page loses on close calls.
- **Trust:** author names, reviews, certifications, a physical address.

## Step 6: Turn gaps into specific changes

Vague advice ("improve content") does not get done. Write each change with its evidence:

> **Add a pricing comparison table under a "Pricing" H2.**
> Evidence: #1 and #2 both have a 4-column price table; our page has no pricing information.

When every recommendation is tied to what a ranking page actually does, it is easier to prioritise and easier to convince a team or client to do the work.

## Step 7: Ship, then measure

Change one page at a time, request indexing, and track the keyword's position for four to eight weeks. If it does not move, the gap is probably authority (links and brand mentions) rather than content.
